KNOW WHAT YOU BREATHE

A clearer view of the air around us.

Air quality can change throughout the day. Here’s a quick guide to the readings EcoWatch follows.

THE MEASUREMENTS

What’s in the air?

PM

PM2.5

Tiny particles 2.5 micrometers wide or smaller. They can travel deep into the lungs and are often linked to traffic, fires, and fuel burning.

PM

PM10

Particles 10 micrometers wide or smaller, including dust, pollen, and smoke. They can irritate the eyes, nose, throat, and lungs.

CO

Carbon monoxide

A colorless gas produced by incomplete combustion. At high concentrations it reduces the oxygen carried through the body.

NO₂

Nitrogen dioxide

A reactive gas mainly released by vehicles and power plants. It can inflame airways and contribute to particle pollution.

O₃

Ground-level ozone

Forms when pollutants react in sunlight. This is not the protective ozone high in the atmosphere; it can make breathing uncomfortable.

AQI

Air Quality Index

A single number that makes air quality easier to understand. Higher values mean greater potential health concern.

THE US AQI SCALE

Reading the colors

EcoWatch uses the US Air Quality Index categories as a simple guide. Local health guidance may differ.

0–50 · GoodAir quality is satisfactory; little or no risk.
51–100 · ModerateAcceptable for most people; unusually sensitive people may be affected.
101–150 · Sensitive groupsChildren, older adults, and people with heart or lung conditions may be affected.
151–200 · UnhealthyEveryone may begin to experience effects; sensitive groups face greater risk.
201–300 · Very unhealthyHealth alert: everyone may experience more serious effects.
301+ · HazardousHealth warning of emergency conditions; follow local public-health advice.

EcoWatch is for awareness and education, not medical advice. Open-Meteo readings are model-based estimates and may not match local monitoring stations. For health decisions, follow guidance from your local authorities.
